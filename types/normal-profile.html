Normal profile

The default shape of a desktop keyboard: full-height switches and tall, sculpted keycaps.

How it works

Normal profile means standard height. The switch stands roughly half a centimetre above the board, and the keycap on top is tall enough that you can see the sides of the keys from the front.

Most keycap sets are sculpted, meaning each row sits at a slightly different height and angle. The tops of the keys then form a shallow curve that follows the fingers, and that curve is most of what a standard keyboard feels like.

This is the profile most office keyboards use, whatever is underneath. A mechanical switch, a rubber dome or a scissor can all sit under the same normal-profile keycap.

The trade-off is height. A normal-profile board is thicker than a low-profile one, so it either needs a taller case or a slight angle to sit comfortably.

Who it suits

Good for

  • Typing at a desk all day
  • A layout that needs no getting used to
  • Standard keycap sets fit
  • Replacing an office keyboard

Less good for

  • Thin, light or portable boards
  • Low desks where total height matters
