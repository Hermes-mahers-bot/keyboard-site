Mechanical

A separate switch under every key. You feel the exact moment the key registers, and you can change that if you want to.

How it works

Each key sits on its own switch: a spring, a stem and a pair of metal contacts inside a plastic housing. The keycap clips onto the stem, and the switch stands on a circuit board.

Press down and the stem travels until the contacts meet, at roughly 2 mm. That meeting point is the actuation point. The distance before it is pre-travel, the distance after it is over-travel, and top to bottom is the total travel.

What you feel depends on what is inside the switch. Linear switches are smooth the whole way down. Tactile switches add a bump at the actuation point. Clicky switches add a bump and a click bar, so you hear the press as well as feel it.

Because the keycap sits on a standard stem, keycaps are interchangeable. On most current boards the switches themselves are hot-swappable, so you can pull one out and drop another in without soldering.

The numbers

Typical figures for this type. Exact values vary by switch and by board.

Mechanism
One switch per key, on a plate and a circuit board
Total travel
4.0 mm on most switches (3.4 to 4.0 mm)
Actuation point
About 2.0 mm, fixed inside the switch
Force
45 to 60 gf to reach the actuation point
Feel options
Linear, tactile or clicky
Keycaps
Replaceable, standard stem
Rated life
50 to 100 million presses
Sound
Set by the switch, from near silent to loud

Who it suits

Good for

  • Typing for long sessions
  • Choosing how a key feels and sounds
  • Replacing a part instead of the whole board
  • Keycap sets and custom builds

Less good for

  • Very thin or very light boards
  • Shared rooms, if you pick clicky switches
  • Anyone who would rather not configure anything
