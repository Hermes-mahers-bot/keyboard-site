Low profile

The same idea as a standard board at half the height. Shorter switches, shorter keycaps, less distance for each press.

How it works

Low profile describes the height of the switch and the keycap, not the mechanism. Low-profile switches exist as mechanical switches, and low-profile rubber-dome keys exist too.

The switch is built shorter, so the keycap sits closer to the board and travels less: about 3 mm from top to bottom instead of 4 mm, with the key registering at around 1.5 mm.

Less travel means less finger movement for every press. Many people find that faster and less tiring. Others miss the feeling of a key going all the way down.

Because the stack is shorter, the whole board can be roughly half the height of a normal-profile one. That is why low-profile keys turn up on laptops, on travel boards and on desks where the keyboard sits flat.

The numbers

Typical figures for this type. Exact values vary by switch and by board.

Total travel
About 3.0 mm (2.5 to 3.4 mm)
Actuation point
About 1.2 to 1.5 mm
Force
Usually lighter than full-height switches, around 35 to 55 gf
Board height
Roughly half that of a normal-profile board
Keycaps
Flat and short, on their own stem; standard sets do not fit
Rated life
50 million presses on most low-profile mechanical switches

Who it suits

Good for

  • Small desks and low setups
  • Typing away from a desk
  • Less finger travel for every press
  • A thinner, lighter board

Less good for

  • Anyone who wants a deep, deliberate press
  • Standard keycap sets
