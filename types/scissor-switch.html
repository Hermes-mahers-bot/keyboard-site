Scissor switch

The mechanism under almost every laptop key. Thin, quiet and level, so the key still goes straight down when you press a corner.

How it works

Two plastic arms cross under the keycap and clip to the rest of the keyboard. They do not carry the signal. They hold the keycap level and let it move only up and down.

The force and the contact come from a rubber dome underneath. Press the keycap and the dome collapses until its conductive pad touches the board, and the key registers. Let go and the dome pushes the keycap back up.

Because the scissor holds all four corners, pressing the edge of a key still moves it straight down. That is what makes a keyboard this thin usable at all.

Travel is short: about 1 to 2 mm in total, with the key registering at roughly 1 mm. There is little to feel and little to hear, which is exactly why laptops and shared offices use it.

The numbers

Typical figures for this type. Exact values vary by switch and by board.

Mechanism
Scissor linkage over a rubber dome
Total travel
About 1.0 to 2.0 mm
Actuation point
About 1.0 to 1.2 mm
Force
About 55 to 70 gf
Sound
Quiet; the dome is most of the noise
Keycaps
Clip on with small hooks, not a standard stem
Rated life
Typically 10 to 20 million presses

Who it suits

Good for

  • Laptops and thin keyboards
  • Quiet rooms and shared offices
  • Pressing keys off centre
  • A flat, low typing position

Less good for

  • Long sessions where you want feedback
  • Swapping or repairing single keys
  • Standard keycap sets
